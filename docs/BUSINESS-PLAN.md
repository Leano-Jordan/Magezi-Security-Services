# Magezi Security Services — Initial Business Plan

**Status:** Early planning draft — not an approved operating plan.  
**Last updated:** 2026-10-20  
**Purpose:** Convert the initial idea into a low-cost, evidence-led plan for a South African contract-guarding business.

## 1. Current direction

- **Proposed business:** Magezi Security Services.
- **Initial service focus:** Contract guarding for businesses and properties.
- **Customer position:** Starting from zero customers. No demand or contract has yet been validated.
- **Startup funding:** Not established. The available amount, source, timing and responsibility for costs are unknown.
- **Owner:** Kabelo Mawelela, based on the CV supplied for review; the legal registration details have not been independently verified.
- **Rosco's role:** Strategic and technical support to Kabelo's company, potentially including planning, branding, web, systems, automation and customer acquisition, in exchange for a small percentage.
- **Arrangement:** The exact percentage and whether it is based on revenue, profit, equity or another measure still need to be agreed. Rosco is not a co-owner by default.
- **Status:** No service contract, ownership split, legal structure, budget, launch date or regulatory status is confirmed.

## 2. Strategy and first milestone

Start narrow. Identify one manageable customer segment and one service model that Magezi can lawfully deliver, staff and finance. Validate demand before spending on a polished website, custom software, uniforms in bulk or other non-essential items.

The first milestone is not company registration or a logo. It is:
1. Both prospective founders understand and accept the proposed working relationship.
2. At least some credible customer demand has been tested.
3. The cost and compliance requirements for a first contract are understood.
4. A realistic plan exists to fund delivery before the customer pays.

## 3. Customer discovery

Initial segments to investigate (not yet selected):
- Small and medium-sized businesses.
- Property managers and commercial property owners.
- Residential complexes or other managed properties.
- Warehouses, workshops or light-industrial premises.

For each prospect, establish:
- What site and guarding problem needs solving?
- Current arrangement and dissatisfaction, if any.
- Required hours, number of guards, duties and supervision.
- Required procurement documents, references, insurance and compliance evidence.
- Decision-maker, procurement process and likely contract start date.
- Budget expectations and payment terms.
- What would make a new provider credible enough to consider?

Do not promise staffing, response times, patrols or other capabilities before operational capacity is confirmed. Interest is not a sale; record concrete next steps and seek evidence rather than relying on encouraging comments.

## 4. Service delivery model — questions to answer

- What exact guarding duties will Magezi offer first?
- Which sites and geographic area can it realistically cover?
- Who recruits, verifies, schedules, supervises and replaces guards?
- What training, registration, uniform, communications and site-specific equipment are required?
- What are the escalation, incident-reporting, relief-cover and complaint procedures?
- What insurance, contracts and health-and-safety arrangements are needed?
- Can Magezi fulfil a contract if a guard is absent, a client pays late or an incident occurs?

Do not accept a contract until the delivery plan and funding are credible.

## 5. Financial model

Build a cost-per-post and contract-level model before quoting any customer.

Include, where applicable:
- Guard wages, overtime, leave, relief cover and statutory employment costs.
- Recruitment, screening, training and required registration.
- Uniforms and equipment.
- Supervision, transport and communications.
- Insurance, compliance, accounting and administration.
- Site mobilisation, contingency and bad-debt / late-payment exposure.
- A sustainable operating margin.

For each potential contract, calculate:
1. Monthly revenue excluding any applicable tax.
2. Full monthly delivery cost.
3. Gross contribution and operating overhead allocation.
4. Working capital needed before the first client payment.
5. Minimum viable price and break-even point.

No prices, startup budget or margins have been established yet. Mark estimates clearly and do not guess them into customer quotations.

## 6. Rosco's support arrangement — open decision

The aim is a clear, fair arrangement that recognises Rosco's contribution while Kabelo builds his company. Keep the agreement simple and proportionate to the work involved.

### Recommended process

1. **Define the percentage.** Agree what the percentage is calculated from, when it is paid or earned, and whether it continues for a set period or ongoing support.
2. **Define contributions.** Write down each person's work, expected hours, accountability, contacts, skills, equipment, cash and financial risk. Distinguish contributions already made from promises about future work.
3. **Run a short, limited planning period.** If both want to explore the idea, use (for example) 30 days to test demand, estimate costs and verify compliance. Set a limit on hours and spending. This is not a reason to demand unlimited unpaid work.
4. **Review the evidence.** Compare actual effort, future responsibilities, risk, available funding and what the business needs.
5. **Choose the structure.** Decide whether the percentage is a service arrangement tied to revenue/profit or actual company shares. Keep the choice straightforward and get professional help if formal share ownership is intended.
6. **Write terms before substantial work.** Record roles, ownership and how it is earned, decision rights, compensation, expense approval, deadlock, departure, dispute handling and IP.

### Principles for fairness

- Do not assume 50/50 is automatically fair, or that unequal equity is automatically fair.
- Do not decide shares based only on who had the idea or who can provide cash today.
- Consider both initial input and ongoing work, responsibility, customer acquisition, operational risk and time commitment.
- Treat equity and payment for work as separate questions. Equity may reward ownership risk; it should not silently become an unlimited substitute for pay.
- If ownership is earned over time or milestones, get professional advice and make the conditions clear.
- Agree what happens if funding or customers do not materialise, or if one person stops contributing.
- Keep Rosscore's reusable tools and IP separate unless a written agreement says otherwise.

Company ownership and a percentage-based service arrangement are different. Confirm legal or tax details with a qualified South African adviser where needed. This section is planning guidance, not legal advice.

## 7. Compliance gate

Before providing security services, verify current requirements directly with PSiRA and the relevant South African authorities. Published PSiRA business-registration guidance has listed CIPC company registration, registration requirements for management/directors at a specified grade, SARS-related documents, a one-year business plan, a declaration of capacity to operate, and immovable office premises among the requirements. Confirm the current checklist, definitions, costs and applicability directly with PSiRA before spending money.

Official starting points:
- PSiRA registration information: https://www.psira.co.za/index.php?catid=2&highlight=WyJjZXJ0aWZpY2F0ZSIsIm9mIiwicmVnaXN0cmF0aW9uIl0%3D&id=77%3Aregistration-division-test1&view=article
- PSiRA registration portal: https://digitalservices.psira.co.za/user-info/9/0

Also confirm, as applicable:
- Registration and standing of the business and each person rendering security services.
- Tax, payroll, UIF, COIDA and employment obligations.
- Insurance and liability cover.
- Applicable labour, health and safety obligations.
- Client-specific procurement requirements and contracts.
- Current employer restrictions or conflicts for the prospective operating partner.

This checklist is a research starting point, not legal advice or proof of compliance. Do not advertise or contract as ready to operate until the requirements are verified and satisfied.

## 8. Lean launch rules

- Validate before spending.
- Keep early choices reversible.
- No custom software until a real operational need is proven.
- No public claim of registration, insurance, qualifications or capability without evidence.
- No contract until staffing, compliance, pricing and working capital are credible.
- Keep written records of decisions, evidence, owners and next actions.

Update this plan only when new information is verified or a decision is explicitly made.


## Director working style

The plan should create momentum, not paperwork for its own sake. Start with the useful opportunity and next action. Add cautions only where they materially affect the decision, and do not overcomplicate everyday choices.
