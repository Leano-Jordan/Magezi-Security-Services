# Magezi Security Services — Initial Business Plan

**Status:** Early planning draft — not an approved operating plan.  
**Last updated:** 2026-10-20  
**Purpose:** Convert the initial idea into a low-cost, evidence-led plan for a South African contract-guarding business.

## 1. Current direction

- **Proposed business:** Magezi Security Services.
- **Initial service focus:** Contract guarding for businesses and properties.
- **Initial market hypothesis:** Local businesses and property operators may need reliable, professional guarding. This must be validated with real customer conversations.
- **Funding constraint:** The prospective operating partner has limited startup funds.
- **Rosco's intended role:** Co-founder with an agreed ownership stake, contributing business strategy, branding, web presence, systems and business development.
- **Operating partner's proposed role:** Security operations and industry expertise.
- **Status:** The venture and partnership are still under discussion. No service contract, ownership split, legal structure, budget, launch date or regulatory status is confirmed.

## 2. Strategy

Start narrow. Identify one manageable customer segment and one service model that Magezi can lawfully deliver, staff and finance. Validate demand before spending on a polished website, custom software, uniforms in bulk or other non-essential items.

The first commercial milestone is a credible, qualified prospect and a realistic understanding of what it would cost to serve them—not simply company registration or a logo.

## 3. Customer discovery

Initial segments to investigate (not yet selected):
- Small and medium-sized businesses.
- Property managers and commercial property owners.
- Residential complexes or other managed properties.
- Warehouses, workshops or light-industrial premises.

For each prospect, establish:
- What site and guarding problem needs solving?
- Current arrangement and dissatisfaction, if any.
- Required hours, number of guards, duties and supervision.
- Required procurement documents, references, insurance and compliance evidence.
- Decision-maker, procurement process and likely contract start date.
- Budget expectations and payment terms.
- What would make a new provider credible enough to consider?

Do not promise staffing, response times, patrols or other capabilities before operational capacity is confirmed.

## 4. Service delivery model — questions to answer

- What exact guarding duties will Magezi offer first?
- Which sites and geographic area can it realistically cover?
- Who recruits, verifies, schedules, supervises and replaces guards?
- What training, registration, uniform, communications and site-specific equipment are required?
- What are the escalation, incident-reporting, relief-cover and complaint procedures?
- What insurance, contracts and health-and-safety arrangements are needed?
- Can Magezi fulfil a contract if a guard is absent, a client pays late or an incident occurs?

Do not accept a contract until the delivery plan and funding are credible.

## 5. Financial model

Build a cost-per-post and contract-level model before quoting any customer.

Include, where applicable:
- Guard wages, overtime, leave, relief cover and statutory employment costs.
- Recruitment, screening, training and required registration.
- Uniforms and equipment.
- Supervision, transport and communications.
- Insurance, compliance, accounting and administration.
- Site mobilisation, contingency and bad-debt / late-payment exposure.
- A sustainable operating margin.

For each potential contract, calculate:
1. Monthly revenue excluding any applicable tax.
2. Full monthly delivery cost.
3. Gross contribution and operating overhead allocation.
4. Working capital needed before the first client payment.
5. Minimum viable price and break-even point.

No prices or margins have been established yet. Do not guess them into customer quotations.

## 6. Partnership principles

Rosco intends to contribute business-building, digital presence, systems and business development in return for an agreed ownership stake. The operating partner is expected to contribute security expertise and lead service delivery. Both sets of responsibilities need confirmation.

Before substantial unpaid work or expenditure, record:
- Initial and ongoing contributions, time commitments and expenses.
- Ownership, vesting/earning conditions if appropriate, decision rights and exit terms.
- Whether either founder is paid for operational or professional work.
- Ownership/licensing of the brand, domain, website, code and reusable Rosscore assets.
- Company/director roles and any regulatory implications.
- What happens if funding, contracts or compliance cannot be secured.

Do not assume that shareholding and directorship are the same thing. Confirm the implications of any proposed role with a qualified South African adviser and PSiRA before finalising the structure.

## 7. Compliance gate

Before providing security services, verify current requirements directly with PSiRA and the relevant South African authorities. The official PSiRA business-registration guidance lists CIPC company registration, registered management/directors at the specified grade, SARS-related documents, a one-year business plan, a declaration of capacity to operate, and immovable office premises among the requirements. Confirm the current checklist, definitions, costs and applicability directly with PSiRA before spending money.

Official starting points:
- PSiRA registration information: https://www.psira.co.za/index.php?catid=2&highlight=WyJjZXJ0aWZpY2F0ZSIsIm9mIiwicmVnaXN0cmF0aW9uIl0%3D&id=77%3Aregistration-division-test1&view=article
- PSiRA registration portal: https://digitalservices.psira.co.za/user-info/9/0

Also confirm, as applicable:
- Registration and standing of the business and each person rendering security services.
- Tax, payroll, UIF, COIDA and employment obligations.
- Insurance and liability cover.
- Applicable labour, health and safety obligations.
- Client-specific procurement requirements and contracts.
- Current employer restrictions or conflicts for the prospective operating partner.

This checklist is a research starting point, not legal advice or proof of compliance. Do not advertise or contract as ready to operate until the requirements are verified and satisfied.

## 8. Lean launch rules

- Validate before spending.
- Keep early choices reversible.
- No custom software until a real operational need is proven.
- No public claim of registration, insurance, qualifications or capability without evidence.
- No contract until staffing, compliance, pricing and working capital are credible.
- Keep written records of decisions, evidence, owners and next actions.

## 9. Current unknowns

- Partner's confirmed qualifications, PSiRA status and availability.
- Which customer segment and site type to target first.
- Exact service scope, staffing plan and geographic coverage.
- Legal structure, registration status and director/management roles.
- Startup funds and who pays each cost.
- Pricing, wages, contract terms and working-capital needs.
- Prospects, customer feedback and likely first contract.
- Ownership split, decision rights, compensation and IP terms.

Update this plan only when new information is verified or a decision is explicitly made.
