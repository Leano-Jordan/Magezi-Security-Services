Magezi Security Services is a proposed local security business. The initial idea is to build a recognisable, easy-to-contact, professional neighbourhood security service that earns trust through reliability and prompt service.

Rosco is exploring a genuine co-founder/strategic partner role. Rosco does **not** want to be treated as a regular admin employee or to keep delivering substantial work indefinitely on vague promises. He wants to explore a fair partnership where contributions, responsibilities, ownership, decision rights and compensation are clear.

This is an opportunity under exploration, not yet an agreed partnership. No ownership percentage, payment, company structure, service offering, launch date, budget or regulatory status has been confirmed.

## 3. The business idea and value proposition

Initial direction:
- Start in the local neighbourhood rather than trying to serve everywhere at once.
- Build a visible, recognisable business that residents can contact easily.
- Compete through professionalism, trust, reliability and prompt service—not through claims the business cannot operationally deliver.
- Start lean, learn what residents actually need, prove demand and confirm operational capacity before investing in custom software or complex systems.

Still to decide:
- Exact services (for example, what kind of guarding, patrol, response, monitoring or other services are legally permitted and operationally realistic).
- Initial service area and target customer groups.
- Operating hours, staffing, vehicles/equipment, communications and escalation procedures.
- What “prompt response” means in practice and what service levels can honestly be promised.
- Pricing, customer contracts, complaint handling, incident reporting and customer acquisition.
- Whether the business has access to the people and resources needed to fulfil what it sells.

These are open questions, not settled offerings. Do not advertise armed response, patrols, monitoring, guaranteed response times or any other service until authorisation, insurance, staff, equipment and delivery capability have been checked.

## 5. Ownership, compensation and fairness

**No ownership split has been agreed.** Any percentages previously discussed are examples only, not a recommendation or promise. Do not treat 50/50, 60/40 or any other split as decided.

Rosco recognises that the operating partner may deserve a larger share because he will carry the security operations and industry responsibilities. At the same time, Rosco's contribution may include substantial setup work and continuing strategic/technical value. The fair arrangement must reflect actual contributions, risk, time, responsibility, capital, client access and future workload—not just who had the original idea.

Before substantial work or spending, discuss and document:
- What each person contributes at the start: cash, time, assets, licences/qualifications, relationships and work already completed.
- Expected weekly/monthly commitment and responsibilities.
- Ownership percentage, vesting or earning conditions if appropriate, and what happens if a person stops contributing.
- Who makes which decisions, what requires both founders' approval, and how disagreements are resolved.
- Whether either person receives wages, service fees, expense reimbursement, dividends, equity, or a combination—and when the business can afford payment.
- Who funds registration, compliance, insurance, equipment, hosting, marketing and other startup expenses.
- Who owns the company, domain, brand assets, website, customer data, code and other intellectual property.
- Whether Rosscore provides ongoing services under a separate agreement and how those services are priced or exchanged for equity.
- Confidentiality, conflicts of interest, exit/departure, share transfers, buyouts, sale of the business, death/incapacity and dispute resolution.

Avoid an informal arrangement where Rosco provides unlimited unpaid work in return for an undefined future stake. Equally, do not pressure the prospective partner into a split before understanding what each person can realistically contribute. A short written founders' term sheet, reviewed by an appropriate South African professional, is a sensible step before major commitments.

## 7. South African legal and operating checks

Requirements depend on the exact services, company structure and operating model. Confirm current requirements directly with the relevant authorities and qualified South African legal/accounting or industry advisers. Items to investigate include:
- CIPC business registration and company administration.
- PSiRA registration and applicable requirements for the business and the people providing security services.
- Firearm licensing and any separate requirements relevant to the proposed activities; an individual's licence must not be assumed to authorise company operations.
- Tax and bookkeeping obligations.
- Appropriate insurance and liability cover.
- Employment, labour, health and safety, training and vetting obligations.
- Local permissions or other requirements relevant to the chosen services.
- Customer contracts, privacy/data handling, incident records and lawful marketing.
- The prospective partner's employment contract, conflicts, confidentiality and any restrictions on outside business activity.

This record does not certify compliance. Do not claim the company is registered, licensed, insured or ready to operate until evidence confirms it. Do not give definitive legal conclusions based only on the current conversation.

## 8. Longer-term Rosscore partnership model

Rosco wants to explore a broader venture-building approach with people he knows in different sectors. In that model, the other partner brings industry-specific expertise and operations; Rosco/Rosscore brings digital systems, technology, branding, business development and strategic execution; the parties negotiate an appropriate ownership stake or compensation.

Treat each venture independently:
- Separate written agreement and ownership records.
- Clear distinction between Rosco personally and Rosscore as a service provider / IP owner.
- Explicit terms for use of code, designs, brands, domains, data and reusable systems.
- No assumption that an interest in Magezi grants rights in any other venture.
- No assumption that Rosco's reusable tools or know-how automatically become Magezi's exclusive property.

Magezi may become a test case for this model, but the model itself is exploratory and should be improved based on experience.
