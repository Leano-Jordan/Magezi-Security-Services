# Magezi Security Services — Foundation & Partnership Baseline

**Status:** Initial discussion; nothing here is a final agreement.  
**Purpose:** Record the starting idea and use it to guide future planning.

## 1. The opportunity

Build a security company focused initially on the local neighbourhood. The idea is to offer security that residents can recognise, contact easily, and rely on for prompt, professional service.

The proposed founder has security-industry experience, is currently employed by another security company, and is understood to have relevant compliance and firearm licensing in place. These details and the requirements for starting an independent business must be confirmed before launch.

## 2. Proposed partnership

The founder would lead security operations and bring industry experience. Rosco would contribute business-building and technical skills, including:

- Company setup and registration coordination
- Logo and brand identity
- Website and online presence
- Business email and social media setup
- Technology, systems, automation and reporting
- Business strategy and ideas for acquiring and serving clients

The intention is to explore co-founding the business, not to treat Rosco as a regular admin employee.

## 3. Ownership and payment

No ownership split or payment arrangement has been agreed.

The founder may have limited startup funds and may not be able to pay for setup work upfront. Rosco has provided informal advice without charge so far.

The parties should discuss and record:
- Each person's responsibilities, time commitment, cash contribution and ongoing work
- A fair ownership split and decision-making rights
- Whether either person will be paid, when the business can afford it, and how expenses are approved
- Whether Rosscore will provide services to Magezi and, if so, under what separate terms
- What happens if a founder stops contributing, leaves, or wants to sell their share

**Working principle:** Be open and fair. Do not promise equity or complete substantial setup work on assumptions alone. Agree on the basic terms in writing before major work or spending begins.

## 4. Immediate proposed work

The following items appear achievable during the first week, subject to agreement and required information:

1. Confirm the business name and registration approach.
2. Develop an initial logo and brand direction.
3. Set up a basic website.
4. Set up a professional business email.
5. Create the appropriate social media accounts.

Keep costs low. Validate demand and operational capacity before investing in custom software or a complex technology stack.

## 5. First business questions

Before launch, clarify:
- What exact security services will be offered?
- Which neighbourhood and customers will be served first?
- How will the service operate, and what response can it realistically promise?
- What startup costs are unavoidable, and who will fund them?
- How will the first paying customers be found?
- What regulatory, insurance, employment and operational requirements apply?

The founder must also ensure that starting a competing business does not breach obligations to their current employer. They should review their employment terms and obtain appropriate advice if necessary.

## 6. Compliance

Before providing security services, confirm the current CIPC, PSiRA, tax, insurance and other applicable requirements for the chosen business structure and services. Do not assume an individual's existing registration or licence automatically authorises a new company to operate.

Use qualified South African legal/accounting advice for ownership documents and regulatory questions where needed.

## 7. Longer-term direction

Rosco wants to explore similar partnerships with capable people in other industries. The potential model is to combine each partner's industry expertise with Rosscore's technology, systems and business-development capabilities, in exchange for a clearly negotiated stake in each venture.

Each partnership should have its own written agreement. Ownership in Magezi does not automatically create ownership in other businesses, and Rosscore's services or intellectual property should be covered by clear terms.

## 8. Next step

Have an informal, honest conversation with the founder. Explain the opportunity and propose exploring a partnership. First understand what he can contribute in time, operations, money and customer acquisition. Then agree on responsibilities and a fair ownership structure before committing to substantial work.
