# Magezi Security Services — Initial Action Plan

**Status:** Proposed actions; owners and deadlines must be confirmed.  
**Last updated:** 2026-10-20  
**Operating rule:** Business planning first; software development only when a validated need exists.

## Current position

- Initial service: contract guarding for businesses or properties.
- Customers: starting from zero; no demand has yet been validated.
- Funding: amount, source and responsibility for costs are unknown.
- Ownership: Magezi is Kabelo's company. Rosco is helping build it in exchange for a small percentage; the exact basis and terms are not yet agreed.

## Priority 1 — Agree the support arrangement

The immediate goal is to agree what Rosco will do and what the small percentage means, in plain language.

- [ ] Confirm that Kabelo owns Magezi and Rosco is providing support, not becoming a co-owner by default.
- [ ] Write down Rosco's key deliverables, time boundaries and any ongoing support expected.
- [ ] Ask what cash is actually available, what it must cover, who can pay which costs, and whether money would be a loan, reimbursable expense or investment.
- [ ] Discuss whether either founder needs payment for work and when the business could realistically afford it.
- [ ] Set a clear limit on unpaid hours and out-of-pocket spending until written terms exist.
- [ ] Check the partner's current employment obligations and possible conflicts before approaching clients or using employer resources.

**Output:** A written summary of shared goals, proposed roles, contributions, unresolved questions and the next decision.

## Priority 2 — Keep the arrangement clear and workable

**Recommended approach:** Agree the scope and percentage basis before substantial work. Keep it simple and fair; avoid open-ended expectations on either side.

- [ ] Agree a limited initial period (for example, 30 days) with specific tasks, time limits and an end-of-period review.
- [ ] Use the period to validate customer demand, estimate startup costs and check compliance—not to provide unlicensed security services.
- [ ] Keep a simple record of hours, deliverables, expenses and introductions made by each person.
- [ ] At the review, compare actual contributions, future responsibilities, financial risk and time commitment.
- [ ] Decide whether a co-founder arrangement is genuinely appropriate. If so, discuss equity that reflects agreed contributions and is earned/retained under clear terms, potentially through a professionally advised vesting or milestone arrangement.
- [ ] Separate ownership from payment for work. Equity is not an automatic replacement for wages, and a promise of future payment is not a budget.
- [ ] Agree how decisions, deadlocks, departure, non-performance, expenses and ownership of brand/domain/website/reusable Rosscore assets will be handled.
- [ ] Put agreed principles into a written, non-binding term sheet for professional review before making binding commitments.

Do not assume 50/50 is automatically fair, and do not assume unequal equity is automatically fair. Compare what each person will actually do, what risk they accept, what they contribute over time and how the business will make decisions.

**Output:** A clear written support-and-percentage arrangement.

## Priority 3 — Establish feasibility and compliance

- [ ] Obtain the current PSiRA business-registration checklist directly from PSiRA.
- [ ] Verify the prospective operating partner's individual registration, grade, standing and relevant qualifications.
- [ ] Use `docs/PARTNER-CREDENTIALS-REVIEW.md` to record evidence and verification status; the intended credential images still need to be reattached and reviewed.
- [ ] Confirm whether proposed directors and people performing executive/management functions must meet specific PSiRA registration requirements.
- [ ] Before deciding whether Rosco should be a director, confirm the regulatory implications. Shareholding and directorship are not the same thing.
- [ ] Confirm CIPC, SARS, payroll, UIF, COIDA, insurance, employment and premises requirements relevant to the intended model.
- [ ] Build a list of setup and ongoing costs, marking each as verified, estimated or unknown.
- [ ] Do not commit to costs until the funding gap and likely first-contract economics are understood.

**Output:** A verified compliance checklist with costs, evidence needed, responsible person and status.

## Priority 4 — Validate customer demand from zero

- [ ] Select one initial customer segment based on the partner's experience and realistic operating capacity.
- [ ] Build a small list of local businesses/property decision-makers using public business contact channels.
- [ ] Speak with prospective buyers about their current guarding needs, contract expectations, procurement requirements, decision process, payment terms and what evidence a new provider must show.
- [ ] Record each conversation, evidence, objections, next step and follow-up date.
- [ ] Do not imply Magezi is already licensed, insured or operational if that has not been verified.
- [ ] Treat positive comments as leads, not proof of demand; seek a concrete next step such as a meeting, procurement checklist or permission to quote once eligible.

**Output:** Evidence of demand, a prioritised prospect list and a clear view of what a first customer would require.

## Priority 5 — Prove contract economics and delivery capacity

- [ ] Define the exact duties, hours, number of guards and geographic coverage for a sample contract.
- [ ] Estimate full staffing costs, relief cover, supervision, equipment, transport, insurance, compliance and overhead.
- [ ] Model the working capital needed to pay costs before the client pays.
- [ ] Establish a minimum viable price and margin before quoting.
- [ ] Draft a service-delivery checklist, incident escalation process and client reporting approach.
- [ ] Confirm the business can cover absence, replacement, supervision and late payment without relying on unverified assumptions.

**Output:** A costed sample contract model and a realistic delivery plan.

## Priority 6 — Spend only after the evidence is in

- [ ] Agree a realistic funding plan and the person responsible for each approved expense.
- [ ] Finalise the founder term sheet with appropriate South African legal/accounting advice.
- [ ] Only then proceed with necessary company setup, brand, domain, business email and a simple website.
- [ ] Do not build bespoke software until a real, recurring business problem justifies it.

**Output:** A lean launch decision backed by founder alignment, compliance, customer evidence and workable economics.
